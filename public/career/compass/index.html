Compass Communications: the job I left because the sales culture made me sick.

My first move to Auckland was personal. Compass Communications ↗ was one of the first jobs I took there, and it lasted only briefly, but two important things came out of it: a very clear line in the sand about how I wanted to sell, and meeting Jack from ALT TV.

The product did not need lies.

Compass had a good proposition. The rates and savings were strong enough that I could sell the switch from Telecom on the numbers themselves.

What infuriated me was the sales culture I encountered. In my recollection, the office sales star was using claims on calls that I believed were plainly deceptive to get customers to switch, and the two managers treated the resulting figures as something close to heroic.

You did not need to lie. I was proving that. I was matching his sales numbers while selling the product honestly, using the real savings and the actual offer. The fact that management could see an honest route producing comparable results and still showed no interest in changing the tactic made the whole thing feel twisted to me.

It was not a minor disagreement over wording. The idea of people getting excited about numbers produced by deceiving customers made me sick to my stomach. I wanted out.

A line I kept

Performance never required me to abandon the customer.

That became an important distinction in the way I built sales systems later. I was intensely commercial and I wanted high numbers, but I wanted the pitch to survive contact with reality. If the product was good enough, the process should make the value clearer, not manufacture value that was not there.

Compass also led directly to ALT TV.

During that short stint I met Jack, a presenter at ALT TV. He asked me to produce the station’s late Friday- and Saturday-night shows. I took them on and rebranded the late-night block as ALT After Dark.

That media connection outlasted the Compass job. I left Compass, moved into HRV, and the ALT TV work continued alongside the new role.

Leaving Compass is what pushed me towards HRV.

I started looking for something else because I did not want to remain in that environment. The opportunity I found next was HRV, and at first I nearly dismissed it because the product sounded painfully dull to me: home ventilation.

That turned out to be one of the more important misjudgements of my career.
