GRID EATER

GRID EATER is where I am bringing together the strongest lessons from 0199, digital services, hosting, business data, direct sales and AI-assisted development.

Why GRID EATER

I wanted a technology brand that could grow beyond one product and beyond New Zealand without carrying the political assumptions of older branding. GRID EATER fits the work: infrastructure, compute, networks, hosting, data and a deliberately hard-edged technical identity.

Business discovery first

The directory side starts with a simple problem: New Zealand businesses are scattered across ageing websites, inconsistent listings and fragmented data sources. I am building systems to organise that information, enrich it and make businesses easier to discover.

Then infrastructure

Once a business relationship exists, the adjacent services are domains, business email, hosting, websites and digital acquisition. That is almost exactly the lesson I learned from 0199, rebuilt for the modern web.

AI changes the economics

AI-assisted development means I can build and maintain far more capable systems with a much smaller team than would have been realistic a few years ago. I see that as an opportunity to lower the cost of modernising New Zealand business websites while putting more value into infrastructure and service.

Human support remains part of the product

I do not believe every customer wants to talk to a bot. Automation should remove repetitive work so a real person can spend more time solving the problem that actually matters.

The long arc

0199 taught me about directory data, telecommunications and recurring customer relationships. The Pākehā Party taught me about distribution, gatekeepers and the power of owning a direct channel. Years of digital work taught me the economics of websites and hosting. GRID EATER is where those lessons are converging into one system.
