Events / Nelson / mid-1990s

Alien Strike Zone.

The first party I designed as an entire world. Before algorithms, YouTube clips or a genre called dubstep, I was trying to make the impossible feel real in my own house.

01 / The reason

I wanted to create an experience, not a business.

I had started putting on house parties in Nelson because I was desperate to play music and increasingly frustrated by the difficulty of holding a band together. DJing offered something different. I could find the records, arrange the sound, choose the people and decide exactly what sort of night I wanted to create.

We had held other parties before, but Alien Strike Zone was the first one I designed around a complete theme. It had to feel different from the moment people arrived. I didn't want a living room with loud music. I wanted a place that seemed to have been taken over by another world.

It was never about money. If a little money arrived, that was simply a consequence of investing a substantial part of my life in the hope of delivering an artistically immersive, perhaps life-changing experience. I wanted to create something people would remember.

02 / The work nobody saw

Days arranging sound, lighting, DJs and the environment.

I spent days thinking about how the party would work as a whole. The DJs had to fit the atmosphere. The sound system and lighting had to serve it. The house and garden had to become part of the experience, not just a background for it.

For Alien Strike Zone I built a crashed UFO, covered areas of the gardens in white sheeting, painted flowers fluorescent colours and lit the property with ultraviolet light. I remember spending about $100 on decorations, not because that was the value of the experience, but because it was what I could make possible with the resources I had.

There was no YouTube to demonstrate how to do any of it, and there was certainly no dubstep scene supplying the soundtrack. This was the mid-1990s. I had my imagination, the music I could find, other people willing to help and the determination to turn an idea into a place you could walk through.

03 / What happened

The reaction I didn't expect.

By my recollection, roughly a hundred people came. We asked for voluntary gold-coin donations, which brought in around $200. But the part I still treasure has nothing to do with that.

Gavin and Murray Kingi both DJed at the party. Murray would later conceive The Gathering, and Gavin was someone I knew through that scene. After the night, Gavin told me, in front of Murray, that it was the most awesome party Nelson had ever seen.

I was profoundly humbled. Hearing that from people I admired, who had just played at something I had built in my own house, meant more than any number on a door takings sheet. I wanted people to have an unforgettable experience. For a moment, it sounded as though I might have managed it.

These details and the compliment are my personal recollections. NZ History documents Murray Kingi's role in The Gathering ↗. This was my third major house party, but the first designed around a complete theme.

Modern videos for a much older ambition.

These recordings were made years after Alien Strike Zone, and the music belongs to later periods. They are not historical footage or tracks I am claiming we played at the party. I use them because the scale, intensity and imagined environments are close to the feeling I tried to construct for real in Nelson.
