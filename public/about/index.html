About David Ruck

I am a Christchurch entrepreneur, sales-systems builder and digital operator. The sectors have changed constantly. The way I work has not: question assumptions, find the real audience and build a better route.

The through-line

I keep ending up where the accepted model is wrong.

That happened in events, where I built audiences outside established channels. It happened in telecommunications, where I challenged a costly sales assumption. It happened in radio, where I pushed a harder sound and argued for a different audience profile. It happened again with 0199, where directory assistance became business data, websites and hosting. GRID EATER is the latest version of the same instinct.

A connected career

I was born in Hartford, Connecticut, in 1977, but Christchurch is the city I grew up in and the place I regard as home. My formative years, early hospitality work, music and rave years, and much of the personal history that shaped me are tied to Christchurch and Canterbury.

My working life has crossed hospitality, direct sales, sales management, telecommunications, call-centre development, gaming, radio, television, live events, directory assistance, business data, websites, domains, hosting, SEO, PPC and artificial intelligence.

Those fields are less disconnected than they look. Kitchens taught me preparation and standards. Music and events taught me attention, timing and audience behaviour. Sales taught me how people decide. Call centres taught me how to turn individual skill into a repeatable system. Telecommunications and 0199 taught me the value of infrastructure and organised business data. Digital work taught me where recurring value really sits. AI now lets me combine those lessons with technical capability that once required a much larger team.

Music came before business.

I started in the Treble Boys’ Choir, moved into drums and rock bands, then into electronic music, DJing, production, house parties and underground raves. One reason DJing suited me was brutally practical: I could find extraordinary music, prepare properly and perform without depending on three other band members to turn up ready.

I never stopped being interested in production, even though I was usually my own harshest critic and kept most of what I made to myself. The media page includes one of the few tracks I put into public circulation →

Sales became systems.

Direct sales became one of the strongest foundations of my career. I became fascinated by why one call converted and another did not: opening lines, questions, objections, offers, follow-up, training and measurement. That eventually became scripting, coaching, pipeline design and outbound and inbound call-centre systems.

Christchurch, Wellington, Auckland, then Christchurch again.

I have moved to Auckland twice. The first move was personal and happened before the post-earthquake years. Later, after the 2011 Christchurch earthquake, I relocated first to Wellington, where 0199 became its own operation, and later spent time in Auckland again before ultimately returning home to Christchurch.

The earthquake period changed the trajectory of my life and work. Wellington became the setting for 0199 Directory Assistance, built around telecommunications, a VOIP call centre, directory data, outbound sales, inbound service and business relationships. When customers then asked for websites, email and hosting, the technology side accelerated.
