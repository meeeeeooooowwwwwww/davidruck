About David Ruck

I am a Christchurch entrepreneur and systems builder. My career has been unusually broad, but the underlying work has been remarkably consistent: sales, audiences, infrastructure, data and finding better ways to connect businesses with customers.

A connected career

I was born in Hartford, Connecticut, in 1977, but Christchurch is the city I grew up in and the place I regard as home. My formative years, early hospitality work, rave and music years, and much of the personal history that shaped me are tied to Christchurch and Canterbury. I returned to Christchurch years ago and have now been based here again for at least a decade.

That context matters because some later media coverage reduced me to an “Auckland businessman”. I did spend part of my career in Auckland, but Auckland was not where my story began and it is not where I live now. After the Canterbury earthquake disruption pushed me out of the central Christchurch environment I had built my life around, I went first to Wellington and later to Auckland. I subsequently returned home to Christchurch, where I have lived again for at least ten years. In my view, describing me simply as an “Auckland businessman” stripped away both my Christchurch roots and the earthquake displacement that explained how I came to be elsewhere in the first place.

My working life has crossed hospitality, direct sales, sales management, telecommunications, call-centre development, gaming, radio, television, live events, directory assistance, business data, websites, domains, hosting, SEO, PPC and artificial intelligence.

Sales taught me how people decide. Call centres taught me how to turn an individual skill into a repeatable system. Telecommunications and 0199 taught me the value of infrastructure and organised business data. Customers asking for websites and email pulled me into digital services. Media and events taught me about attention and audiences. AI now lets me combine those lessons with technical capability that once required a much larger team.

Learning standards early

Before technology became central to my career, I worked in junior and trainee chef roles in respected South Island restaurants. What stayed with me was the standard: excellent work normally looks effortless only because somebody did an enormous amount of preparation behind the scenes.

Sales became systems

Direct sales became one of the strongest foundations of my career. I became fascinated by why one call converted and another did not: opening lines, questions, objections, offers, follow-up, training and measurement. That eventually became scripting, coaching, pipeline design and building outbound and inbound call-centre systems.

The earthquake changed my trajectory

The Canterbury earthquake period was not an abstract news event in my biography. Christchurch was home. The earthquakes and the loss of normal access to the central city disrupted my life and work badly enough that I was pushed out of the environment I had built my life around.

After the 2011 Christchurch earthquake I relocated first to Wellington. That move became the setting for one of the most important business chapters of my life: 0199 Directory Assistance. I developed a four-digit directory-assistance service around telecommunications, a VOIP call centre, directory data, sales, customer service and business relationships. I later spent time in Auckland, but that came after Wellington in the post-earthquake sequence. Ultimately, Christchurch became home again and has been for at least the last decade.

When 0199 customers asked us for websites, business email and hosting, the next move became obvious.

The public story is more complicated

In 2013 I created the Pākehā Party Facebook page. It became a national controversy at extraordinary speed. From my recollection, its viral growth was already underway organically before mainstream media coverage began. The page's audience later overtook established political parties in social-media popularity within days, and my name became attached to a political and media narrative that still dominates some search results more than a decade later.

I am not asking anyone to forget that period or agree with my interpretation of it. I am asking that my own account exists alongside everybody else's.
