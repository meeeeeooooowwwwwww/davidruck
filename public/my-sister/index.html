Story One

You know what happened

Editorial note: This is my first-person account of events I experienced and of what I was later told other people said or believed. Where I describe motives or responsibility, I am stating my own conclusion, not pretending that a court has ruled on it.

By 2016, years after the Pākehā Party media firestorm had first turned my name into a public target, I supported Donald Trump. In my account, the hostility and reputational damage that had accumulated around me did not simply disappear when the headlines moved on. It followed me into the next chapter of my life.

I say that gangs later came to my home and business premises and tried to force me to sign over my company. I say I was threatened with guns. I also witnessed the consequences of serious sexual violence against a young woman who was present. Those are not colourful details added years later for effect. They are part of what I say happened to me during the period when my life was already collapsing under years of hostility, isolation and public caricature.

What came afterwards is one of the reasons this page exists.

I later learned that my sister, Denise, had been told by a friend that the real explanation was supposedly much simpler: that I had brought the trouble on myself because I owed gangs money for drugs. She accepted that version and, as I understand it, repeated it to other people.

I reject that story completely. At the time I was still receiving substantial income from hosting, SEO and Google Ads management. Whatever else was going wrong around me, I made a point of paying what I owed. I did not leave debts hanging over me. The suggestion that the violence I describe was merely the predictable result of an unpaid drug debt is, in my view, a convenient story that converts a victim into the author of his own destruction.

What hurt most was not that somebody said something ugly about me. After years in the media spotlight, ugly stories were hardly new. It was that my own family could accept an explanation that placed the blame back on me without first sitting down with me, looking at what I was actually earning, asking what happened, or considering whether the people feeding them that story might themselves be wrong.

I felt abandoned by my family. I felt abandoned by the police. I felt that I had been left to rot inside a version of my own life constructed largely by other people, while the people closest to me found it easier to accept explanations that required nothing from them.

For years I swallowed that. I watched people avoid me, refuse to speak to me, repeat versions of events I believe are false and then use the distance they themselves created as proof that there must be something wrong with me.

I am done with that arrangement.

The people involved will recognise the events I am talking about. They know what conversations were had. They know what they chose to believe and what they repeated. They also know that for years I was the person who stayed quiet.

I am not staying quiet anymore. I will document what I experienced, what I can prove, what I was told, where the stories conflict, and where people who judged me have refused even to sit across a table from me and explain themselves.

This is only the beginning.

David Ruck
