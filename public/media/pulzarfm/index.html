Radio · Sales · Christchurch

PulzarFM

At PulzarFM I was Sales Manager and also took over the Friday and Saturday late night dance music shows. There was very little client history, almost no sales infrastructure and no real budget, so the job was a mixture of building revenue from scratch, bringing inexperienced people along for the ride, and then spending the weekends pushing the station much harder on air.

The Monday meeting

Part of the appeal of getting DJs to come up and bring friends with them was simple: energy. If the DJ was genuinely having a good night, surrounded by friends and a small party in the studio, that energy came through in the performance and therefore through the radio. The shows sounded alive because the room actually was alive.

That did create one recurring problem. We drank in the studio. People smoked. It was Friday and Saturday night, the window was open, and my theory was that most of the evidence would have disappeared by the time the weekday shows returned on Monday.

Apparently not.

Almost every Monday Andy would pull me into his office and say, “David, you cannot drink and smoke in the studio.”

I would say, “Yeah. Sorry about that.”

I genuinely accepted the telling-off, but I also knew the party atmosphere was a big part of why the shows worked, so I was happy to wear the Monday lecture on behalf of everyone who came in and helped create them.

Eventually I decided to get ahead of it. One Monday morning, before Andy arrived, I quickly printed several signs and stuck them around the studio:

NO SMOKING. NO ALCOHOL. NO SWEARING.

Andy came in, saw them, and had to laugh. I do not remember getting another Monday lecture after that.

Put it on Carolyn’s tab.

Working as Sales Manager at PulzarFM was not a conventional sales-management job. The station had very little client history, no established sales team and practically no budget. Most of the time it was me, plus whoever had decided they wanted to give radio sales a go.

One week Andy Poulsen called me into his office and pointed at a pile of mail.

“You know what that is David?”

“No.”

“It’s bills.”

“Who’s Bill?”

We needed roughly $3,000 that week or things were going to get uncomfortable. Andy mentioned Carolyn, who owned venues on the old Christchurch Strip including Ferment and Liquidity. PulzarFM already had a small arrangement with her, around $500 a month, and it was coming up for renewal.

I called her and arranged to meet after lunch service. I remember the meeting as being at Ferment.

While arranging it she made one thing very clear: “You guys can’t have a tab at the bar. I gave MediaWorks a tab and they completely ruined my bar.”

I took Miles with me, one of the inexperienced guys who sometimes came along on sales calls. We arrived and the staff said Carolyn would be down in about ten minutes. That made her roughly five minutes late.

So I ordered two lagers.

When the bartender asked me to pay, I said, “They go on Carolyn’s tab.”

Carolyn walked in at almost exactly that moment.

I opened with: “You’re late so I got me and Miles here a drink on your tab due to the fact that you said we couldn’t have one.”

She laughed, called me cheeky, and we sat down.

I put a contract in front of her for $5,000 a month for three months and asked for the first month up front by cheque.

She signed it and wrote the $5,000 cheque there and then.

Miles looked stunned. I was fairly impressed myself.

I went back to the station, put the cheque next to the pile of bills on Andy’s desk and he looked at it.

“How’d you manage THAT!?”

I said, “You own a radio station Andy. How do you NOT manage that.”

Then I left for the day.

After Dark

On Friday and Saturday nights I took over the late slots and pushed them much harder. Friday leaned into harder electro and house from around 10pm through until dawn. Saturday moved further into dubstep and drum & bass.

I also liked bringing guest DJs into the station, including people who were not necessarily part of the established networks. They could come in, play live and actually be heard.

My view at the time was that the shows became the station’s strongest by a wide margin. Andy believed the audience was predominantly female; I thought it was overwhelmingly young and male. Later public material described PulzarFM’s target audience as roughly 75/25 male to female, which was much closer to what I was seeing.
