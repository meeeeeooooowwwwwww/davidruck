Media & Culture

Radio, Television, Events & Christchurch Culture

Long before digital business, I was learning how audiences form in restaurants, parties, rave events, radio and television. Surreal Entertainment began while I was still very young in Christchurch.

Surreal Entertainment started in my youth

I founded Surreal Entertainment while I was still very young and working in Christchurch restaurants. It did not begin later in Auckland and it was not an offshoot of my television work.

I had started putting on parties. They grew. As more people turned up, small informal spaces stopped being enough, so I began finding larger venues, hiring better sound and production equipment and charging admission so the events could scale properly. What began as parties became event promotion and Surreal Entertainment grew out of that process.

That period taught me lessons I have used ever since: how word of mouth compounds, how a strong identity creates its own audience, how production quality changes what people will pay for, and how quickly something can move from a small idea to an operation requiring venues, suppliers, security, equipment, promotion and thousands of people.

Large Christchurch rave events

The parties continued to grow into substantial rave events. One large event, which I remember drawing roughly 3,000 people, later became part of the context behind the worst criminal mistake of my youth. I address that openly rather than pretending it has nothing to do with my story.

PulzarFM 105.7

At Christchurch dance-music station PulzarFM 105.7 I later worked as Sales Manager while also presenting late-night rave radio. It connected advertising, business relationships, audience development, nightlife and music culture.

Funk & Fish & Chips

I became involved in repositioning a struggling takeaway as a colourful late-night destination built around strong fast food, arcade games, street art, rave music and a DJ setup. The earthquake ended the business, but the experience reinforced how much identity and positioning matter.

ALT TV came later

My television work came much later. I worked as a Producer at ALT TV in Auckland, adding television production and broadcast experience to skills I had already developed through events, promotion, suppliers, live audiences, radio and sales.

Surreal Entertainment predates ALT TV by years. It belongs to the early Christchurch part of my story, alongside the restaurant and rave years, not to the later Auckland television chapter.
