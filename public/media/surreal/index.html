Surreal

Surreal was a brand rather than a formally constituted company. It grew out of the same instinct that started with house parties in Nelson: create something visually memorable, promote it physically, and give people a reason to show up.

Before Surreal, there were house parties.

While I was working in hospitality in Nelson I had started DJing and putting on increasingly elaborate parties. We brought in local bands and DJs, transformed the house with whatever materials we could find, and promoted the events by walking around town handing out flyers.

The third major party, Alien Strike Zone, drew roughly a hundred people. I spent about $100 on decorations, built a crashed UFO, covered gardens in white sheeting, painted flowers with fluorescent colour and lit the property with UV lights. A voluntary gold-coin donation brought in roughly $200.

Murray Kingi, who went on to conceive The Gathering ↗, brought DJ equipment and several high-profile Gathering DJs. Their reaction helped convince me that the formula could scale. NZ History records the first Gathering and Murray’s role ↗

Back in Christchurch, that instinct became Surreal.

I started using Surreal as the brand around underground rave events in empty buildings. There was no formal company behind it. There was a name, a rapidly growing crowd, physical promotion and a lot of improvisation.

Promotion was flyers, streets, schools, record shops and word of mouth. The events grew into the thousands, and the production became more serious as the audience grew.

The night police found more than 2,000 kids inside

At one event uniformed police found the party while more than 2,000 people were inside. I expected the whole thing to be shut down.

Instead, one officer said: “We were wondering where all the kids were tonight.”

They looked around, decided things appeared to be under control and left us to continue. The event ran until about 6am.

What Surreal taught me

Audience building happened in the physical world.

There were no social platforms doing the distribution for us. If I wanted two thousand people in a room, someone had to design the idea, create the identity, distribute the flyers, recruit helpers, convince suppliers and make the night work.

There is considerably more to document about individual Surreal events, venues, collaborators and the later consequences around one of the largest parties. This page is the canonical home for that story as it grows.
